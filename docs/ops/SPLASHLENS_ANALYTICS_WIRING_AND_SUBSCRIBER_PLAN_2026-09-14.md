## Next Subscriber Attack Plan

1. Make the first screen a 60-second field challenge.
   - Primary copy: `Try one code, part, or equipment family.`
   - Route users into lookup or PartSnap immediately.
   - End every successful result with `Helpful`, `Wrong`, `Missing info`, and `Saved me time`.

2. Push PartSnap harder after open.
   - Add a first-run prompt that asks for one proof photo or code.
   - Track `partsnap_started`, `partsnap_result`, `partsnap_feedback`, and `partsnap_paywall_seen`.

3. Move the paid CTA to the moment after value.
   - Do not sell before the first useful result.
   - After a useful lookup or scan, show Pro as saved history, more scans, proof packet, and customer/vendor summary.

4. Build named pilot tracking.
   - 25 individual techs.
   - 5 service companies.
   - 3 facility or training organizations.
   - Each gets a tracked link, a 14-day task, and a follow-up ask.

5. Import official store metrics daily.
   - App Store Connect installs, product page views, conversion rate.
   - Google Play installs, store listing visitors, acquisition source, retention.
   - Store summaries should land in D1 as `store_metric_import` rows, separate from app telemetry.

6. Keep outreach focused on one measurable ask.
   - `Try one real code, part, or equipment family and tell me where it falls short.`
   - Every lane gets its own tracked URL so press, trainers, manufacturers, and service companies can be compared.

## Bottom Line

The analytics pipe is now wired across app, site, Cloudflare, D1, and Amplitude. The project is no longer blind on site traffic. The next problem is product activation: too many people open or click, too few complete a first useful workflow, and nobody is currently reaching checkout intent in the clean 30-day funnel.
